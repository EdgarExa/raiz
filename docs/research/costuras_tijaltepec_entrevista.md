# 📋 Field Pain Validation Sheet: Interview #02

## 1. General Info

- **Community:** San Pablo Tijaltepec, Oaxaca
- **Artisan:** Clothing and textile producer
- **Native Language:** Spanish

## 2. The Economic Pain & Injustice (Real Numbers)

- **Craft piece:** Traditional embroidered garments and textiles.
- **Time invested:** The artisan explains that embroidery takes a long time to complete.
- **Out-of-pocket material cost:** Not specified in the interview.
- **Price paid by the middleman:** The artisan receives approximately 20–30% of the final selling price.
- **Price the middleman resells it for:** According to the artisan, resellers can increase the price by 80–100%.
- **Fair price the artisan asks for:** The artisan wants fair compensation for the time and work invested in each piece.

## 3. Specific Abuses Reported

**Question:** Have you ever felt that your design, pattern, or technique was copied without your permission or recognition?

**Answer:** Yes. The artisan says that there is a lot of imitation nowadays and that other people want to make products similar to theirs.

---

**Question:** How do you currently prove that your design belongs to you and that you created it first?

**Answer:** Mainly through social media. The artisan publishes the garments and products to show that they were made in their community.

---

**Question:** How do you determine or calculate the price of a piece? Do you consider the time required to make it?

**Answer:** Yes. The artisan considers the amount of time required to make the embroidery, but explains that intermediaries pay very little and later sell the pieces at much higher prices.

---

**Question:** Do you depend on intermediaries to sell your products?

**Answer:** Yes. The artisan currently needs intermediaries to sell the products, but would like to organize with other producers and find markets where they can sell their products at better prices.

## 4. Cultural Plagiarism

**Question:** Have you noticed that other people sell or imitate products or designs made in your community?

**Answer:** Yes. The artisan says that there is a lot of imitation and that some garments made in San Pablo Tijaltepec are published and sold by other people without clearly recognizing the origin of the work.

**Problem identified:** The artisan wants to have a recognizable brand so that each product can be identified with its original creator and community.

## 5. Digital Access & Market Problem

**Question:** How do you currently find buyers? Do you depend on fairs, events, or personal contacts?

**Answer:** Currently, the artisan finds buyers mainly through personal contacts. They would like to participate in fairs and events, such as the Guelaguetza, but do not have easy access to these events.

---

**Question:** Would you be interested in having a verified digital profile with your pieces, techniques, and contact information?

**Answer:** Yes. The artisan is interested because it would allow people to know the origin of their textiles and their work.

---

**Question:** Do you have a constant internet connection in your workshop or place of sale?

**Answer:** No. The connection is not constant or good quality because the area has poor signal.

## 6. Proposed Digital Solution

**Question:** Which of the ideas I mentioned do you think would solve a real problem for your workshop?

**Answer:** The artisan is mainly interested in a digital tool to showcase their embroidery and sewing, reach more buyers, and sell products faster. They are also interested in having their own brand to identify the origin of each product and protect the identity of their work.

## 7. Audio Testimony in Spanish
- **Spanish translation:** The artisan explains that local producers receive very little money compared with the prices charged by resellers. They are interested in a digital platform that allows them to showcase their embroidery, reach more buyers, sell products faster, and establish a brand that identifies the original source of their textiles and work.
